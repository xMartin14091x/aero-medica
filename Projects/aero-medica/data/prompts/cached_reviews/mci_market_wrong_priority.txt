Overall Assessment
The Mass Casualty scenario was completed with significant triage and prioritisation errors. Six patients were present -- Malee, Tawan, and Chai (RED), Lek and Kanda (GREEN), Boonsri (BLACK) -- but treatment order was incorrect and multiple triage tags were wrong. Protocol adherence: approximately 38%. 3 critical errors detected. In an MCI, correct triage determines who lives and who dies.

Strengths
- Most patients were approached and received some assessment
- Some treatments were applied correctly
- The scenario was completed within the time limit
- Equipment was accessed and used

Areas for Improvement
- GREEN patients (Lek and/or Kanda) were treated before RED patients (Malee, Tawan, Chai) -- this is a fundamental triage violation
- Boonsri may have been incorrectly triaged as RED instead of BLACK -- in an MCI, a patient in cardiac arrest with no pulse and no breathing is expectant (BLACK), not immediate (RED)
- Tawan was unconscious with obstructed airway but received delayed or no airway management
- Chai had the highest bleeding severity (severity 3) but was not prioritised among the RED patients
- Malee's bleeding was identified but not controlled effectively
- No initial triage sweep was performed -- patients were assessed in encounter order rather than priority order

Critical Errors
- GREEN patients treated before RED patients -- in MCI, RED (immediate life threat) must always come before GREEN (walking wounded)
- Boonsri incorrectly triaged -- cardiac arrest in MCI = BLACK (expectant). Attempting CPR on Boonsri while Malee, Tawan, and Chai need immediate care costs lives
- Tawan's obstructed airway not managed -- unconscious patient with breathing rate of 8/min and obstructed airway will die without intervention

Recommendations
- Before treating anyone in an MCI: walk past every patient in 60 seconds and classify them. Can they walk? Are they breathing? What is their pulse?
- Remember the MCI rule: do not perform CPR when other salvageable patients need immediate care -- BLACK means expectant, not abandoned
- Practice the START triage algorithm until the classification is automatic: walk/breathe/pulse -- that is all you need for initial triage
- Repeat this scenario focusing only on correct triage order before worrying about treatment completeness
